Houston theft defense

A theft conviction follows you to every job application. Many theft cases can be reduced, diverted or dismissed.

What counts as theft

Under Texas Penal Code § 31.03, theft means taking property without the owner's effective consent, intending to deprive them of it. That includes shoplifting, theft of services, embezzlement, receiving stolen property and borrowing something you never meant to return. If force or threats are involved, the charge becomes robbery.

Theft is graded by value

Value of propertyLevelRange
Under $100Class C misdemeanorFine up to $500
$100 to under $750Class B misdemeanorUp to 180 days in jail, fine up to $2,000
$750 to under $2,500Class A misdemeanorUp to 1 year in jail, fine up to $4,000
$2,500 to under $30,000State jail felony180 days to 2 years, fine up to $10,000
$30,000 to under $150,000Third-degree felony2 to 10 years, fine up to $10,000
$150,000 to under $300,000Second-degree felony2 to 20 years, fine up to $10,000
$300,000 or moreFirst-degree felony5 to 99 years or life, fine up to $10,000

Prior theft convictions and certain victims, like the elderly, can raise the level.

Defenses

  • No intent to deprive the owner
  • Consent, ownership or a good-faith claim to the property
  • Mistaken identity and unclear surveillance video
  • Challenging the claimed value, which can drop a felony to a misdemeanor
  • Abandoned property, alibi or insufficient evidence

For a first offense, pretrial diversion or deferred adjudication may keep a theft off your record, which matters because a theft conviction is treated as a crime of dishonesty by employers.
