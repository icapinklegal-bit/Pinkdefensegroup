Criminal law FAQ

Straight answers to the questions we hear most. For advice about your case, call us.

Do I really need a lawyer for a criminal case?

Yes. Your freedom and record are at stake. Get counsel before an indictment if you can, and never talk to law enforcement without one.

If I did it, do I still need a lawyer?

Yes. A lawyer can find legal problems with the stop, search or evidence, challenge what the State can prove, and negotiate a far better outcome than you'd get alone.

What is an indictment?

The formal charging document in a felony case, returned by a grand jury that found probable cause. In misdemeanor cases, the charging document is called an information.

Police never read me my rights. Is my case dismissed?

Not automatically. Miranda warnings are required before custodial interrogation. Statements taken without them may be suppressed, but volunteered statements can still be used, and the rest of the case continues.

When can police search me, my home or my car?

With a warrant, within its scope; incident to a lawful arrest, covering you and the area within your reach; with consent; and in other limited situations. Items in plain view may be seized. A legally towed car can be inventoried, but police can't impound a car just to search it.

How long will my case take?

There's no set timeline. Cases take from a few months to a few years, depending on lab results, witnesses, plea negotiations and co-defendants.

Will my case go to trial?

Probably not. More than 90 percent of state cases end without a trial, through dismissal, reduction or plea. Being ready to try the case is what produces good results without one.

Can I get a charge off my record?

Often. Dismissals, acquittals and completed deferred adjudication may qualify for expunction or nondisclosure. See our record sealing page.

I'm innocent. Can I just explain things to the police?

Please don't. Innocent people's statements get taken out of context, and nerves cause mistakes. Talk to a lawyer first.

What's the difference between a misdemeanor and a felony?

Misdemeanors carry up to a year in county jail. Felonies carry state jail or prison time, up to life or death for capital felonies. See the full ranges on our criminal defense page.

What's the difference between state and federal crimes?

Federal cases usually involve interstate activity, federal property, federal agencies or federal funds. They follow different rules and sentencing. See federal crimes.

I just found out I have a warrant. What do I do?

Call a lawyer before you do anything else. We can advise on bond options and, in some cases, arrange to have the warrant handled without you sitting in jail.
