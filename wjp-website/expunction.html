Expunction & record sealing

A dismissed case can still cost you a job. We can often clear it.

Two ways to clear a record

Expunction destroys the records of an arrest. It's generally available when a case was dismissed, you were acquitted, or charges were never filed, and for some Class C offenses after deferred disposition.

An order of nondisclosure seals a record from most public view, including most employers and landlords. It's often available after you successfully complete deferred adjudication, and for some first-time misdemeanors.

Why it matters even without a conviction

An arrest that was dismissed can still show up on background checks for jobs, apartments and professional licenses. Clearing it lets you legally deny the arrest in most situations.

Waiting periods and eligibility

Eligibility depends on the offense, the outcome and your history, and some cases have waiting periods. Bring us your case information and we'll tell you whether you qualify and when.
