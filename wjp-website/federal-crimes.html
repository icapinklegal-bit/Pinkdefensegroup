Houston federal criminal defense

Federal prosecutors win most of their cases before trial. Your defense should start before the indictment.

When a case becomes federal

Federal charges come from conduct that crosses state lines, involves a federal agency or federal money, happens on federal property, or violates a federal statute. Houston cases are prosecuted in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Texas at the Bob Casey Courthouse, one of the busiest federal trial courts in the country.

Federal cases we handle

  • Drug trafficking and distribution, including multi-agency and cartel-linked investigations
  • Federal firearms offenses, often charged alongside drug cases
  • Wire fraud, mail fraud, money laundering and embezzlement
  • Illegal reentry and human smuggling
  • Identity theft, computer fraud and other cybercrimes

Investigating agencies include the FBI, DEA, ATF, IRS Criminal Investigation and Homeland Security Investigations.

Call before you're indicted

Federal agents often build a case for months using wiretaps, informants, financial records and surveillance before anyone is arrested. If you've received a target letter, been contacted by an agent or had a search warrant executed, that is the time to hire counsel.

The federal process

  1. Investigation

    Agents gather evidence, often for months or years.

  2. Grand jury

    A panel of 16 to 23 citizens decides whether to indict. The defense is not in the room.

  3. Arraignment and detention hearing

    The government may ask to hold you until trial. Protecting your release is an early priority.

  4. Discovery and motions

    We review the government's evidence and move to suppress anything obtained unlawfully.

  5. Plea or trial

    Most federal cases end in a plea agreement. The terms of that agreement, and the sentencing guideline calculation, are where good defense work pays off.

Federal sentencing

There is no parole in the federal system, so defendants serve at least 85 percent of the sentence imposed. Many drug and firearm offenses carry mandatory minimums, and federal gun sentences often run consecutively. A defense lawyer can challenge the guideline calculation, pursue reductions and, in the right case, negotiate cooperation that allows a sentence below the mandatory minimum. One client facing nine years left with five.

Common questions

Can I be charged federally after my state case was dismissed? Yes. The state and federal governments are separate sovereigns, so double jeopardy doesn't prevent it.

Can the government take my property before I'm convicted? It can try, through civil asset forfeiture, which runs separately from the criminal case and should be fought separately.
