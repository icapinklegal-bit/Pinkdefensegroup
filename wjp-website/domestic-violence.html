Houston domestic & family violence defense

One accusation can cost you your home, your children and your gun rights before you ever see a jury. We move fast.

What Texas calls family violence

Texas law uses the term “family violence.” It covers people related by blood or marriage, members of the same household, and people who are or were in a dating relationship, even if they never lived together.

What happens right after the arrest

Family violence cases move fast. A judge, not a standard bail schedule, sets bond. An emergency protective order is often issued at the first appearance and can keep you out of your own home and away from your children for 31 to 91 days. Violating it, even accidentally, is a separate crime. Getting a lawyer involved before that hearing can shape the conditions you live under for months.

What you should do now

  • Have no contact with the complainant, directly or through others.
  • Write down your account while it's fresh.
  • Save texts, voicemails, photos and anything else that shows what really happened.
  • Stay off social media.
  • Talk about the case only with your lawyer. Other conversations are not privileged.

Defenses

  • Self-defense. Texas law allows force to protect yourself. Several of our dismissals involved clients who were the ones attacked.
  • False or exaggerated allegations. Many cases rest almost entirely on one person's word. We test it against the evidence.
  • Non-cooperation. A complainant can't drop the case, but their position matters, and we know how to present it.
  • Investigation errors. Unlawful arrests and improper procedures can lead to suppressed evidence.
  • Enhancements. We challenge prior convictions used to raise a misdemeanor to a felony.

The long-term stakes

Federal law permanently bars anyone convicted of misdemeanor domestic violence from owning a firearm. A conviction can also affect custody, housing, employment, professional licenses and immigration status.
