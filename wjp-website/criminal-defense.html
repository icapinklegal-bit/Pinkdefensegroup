Houston criminal defense attorneys

Misdemeanor to capital felony, state and federal. Here's what to do now and how your case will move.

What a defense lawyer does from day one

A good defense starts long before trial. From the first call, we look at how police gathered the evidence and what the witnesses actually saw. We talk to prosecutors before the first hearing and appear at every setting from arraignment through resolution. The earlier we are involved, the more we can change.

Protect yourself before you say another word

  • Stay silent. Identify yourself, then ask for a lawyer. Jail calls are recorded and prosecutors use them.
  • Write it down. While your memory is fresh, note what happened, the officers involved and anyone who saw it.
  • Save the evidence. Keep texts, photos, receipts and arrest and bond paperwork. Nearby camera footage is often overwritten within days.
  • Call a defense attorney now, not after your first court date.

How a Texas criminal case moves

  1. Arrest and bond

    A magistrate sets bail. We can ask the court to lower bond or change release conditions.

  2. First appearance

    The charges are read and a plea is entered. If bail wasn't set earlier, the judge sets it here.

  3. Pretrial

    We review the State's evidence, file motions to suppress and negotiate. Many cases are dismissed or reduced at this stage.

  4. Trial

    The State must prove every element beyond a reasonable doubt. If the case should be tried, we try it.

The courts we work in

Harris County: felonies are heard in 24 criminal district courts and misdemeanors in 16 county criminal courts at law, at the Criminal Justice Center, 1201 Franklin Street.

Fort Bend County: felonies go to district courts including the 240th, 268th and 400th, and misdemeanors to the county courts at law at the Justice Center in Richmond. We represent clients from Sugar Land, Missouri City and Rosenberg.

We also handle cases in Brazoria, Galveston, Montgomery and other Texas counties, and in federal court.

Consequences beyond the sentence

A conviction can affect your job, professional license, housing and right to own a firearm. A felony suspends your right to vote until the sentence is fully discharged. For non-citizens, many convictions carry immigration consequences. Some cases can be sealed or erased afterward through expunction or nondisclosure.
