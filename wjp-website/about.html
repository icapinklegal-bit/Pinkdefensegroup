From the boxing ring to the courtroom

How a heavyweight boxer and college professor built a Houston law firm that has defended Texans for more than 50 years.

The Tobasco Kid

Before he was a lawyer, Walter Jerome Pink was a heavyweight boxer. They called him “The Tobasco Kid,” and he was known for the power in his hands. He was also a college professor, teaching by the age of 28.

Then came a single day that changed everything. He witnessed a student arrested wrongfully and illegally. That same day, his wife, Betty Pink, was discriminated against at a business. He came away convinced that the fights that mattered most for his community would be won or lost in the legal system, and he went to law school to fight them.

In his own words, he traded his boxing gloves for the “gloves of freedom and justice.”

A career in the courtroom

Walter J. Pink became known in Houston as a civil rights advocate and a lawyer who would take on any fight. Over more than three decades of practice, he:

  • handled more than 20,000 criminal cases
  • tried more than 600 criminal cases to verdict
  • was formally recognized by the Texas Senate for his work

Three generations

  1. Walter J. Pink, founder

    Boxer, professor and civil rights lawyer who built the firm on trial work and refusing to back down.

  2. Augustin T. Pink and Clement B. Pink

    His sons. Augustin, licensed in 1992, leads the criminal defense practice. Clement leads civil litigation and personal injury.

  3. Ishmael G.A. Pink

    His grandson. A two-time University of Texas graduate who rose from law clerk to partner, with a focus on DWI and serious criminal charges.

The firm today

Walter J. Pink & Associates, P.C. practices from 1717 St. James Place, Suite 690 in Houston's Galleria area. Across five decades, the family has handled more than 25,000 cases and more than 600 trials. We take criminal cases on a flat fee, we answer the phone, and we still believe what the founder believed: every case is worth fighting.
