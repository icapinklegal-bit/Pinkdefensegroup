Houston burglary defense

Burglary of a home is a felony carrying up to 20 years, even if nothing was taken.

What the State has to prove

Under Texas Penal Code § 30.02, burglary means entering a home, or a building not open to the public, without the owner's consent and with intent to commit a felony, theft or assault. It also covers hiding inside with that intent, or entering and then committing or attempting one of those crimes. Nothing has to be taken; the offense is complete on entry.

Types of burglary and penalties

ChargeLevelRange
Burglary of a vehicleClass A misdemeanorUp to 1 year in jail; minimum 6 months with a prior
Burglary of a buildingState jail felony180 days to 2 years, fine up to $10,000
Burglary of a habitationSecond-degree felony2 to 20 years, fine up to $10,000
Burglary of a habitation intending a felony other than theftFirst-degree felony5 to 99 years or life
Criminal trespassClass B or Class A misdemeanorUp to 180 days or up to 1 year

A habitation includes houses, apartments, hotel rooms and anything used for sleeping overnight, like an RV. Breaking into a pharmacy or a building storing controlled substances raises a building burglary to a felony with up to 10 years.

Defenses

  • Consent to be there, including a right to enter a shared home
  • No intent to commit a crime inside, which may reduce the charge to criminal trespass
  • Mistaken identity and weak forensic evidence
  • Duress or necessity

In one of our cases, a client was locked out of his own home and then accused of breaking in to attack the complainant. Both the burglary and assault charges were dismissed.
