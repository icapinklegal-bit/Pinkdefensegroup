Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle cases in both Harris County and Fort Bend County?

Yes. We regularly appear at the Harris County Criminal Justice Center in downtown Houston and the Fort Bend County Justice Center in Richmond, as well as municipal courts in Houston, Sugar Land, Missouri City, Stafford, Rosenberg and Katy, and federal court in the Southern District of Texas.

How is PinkDefense related to Walter J. Pink & Associates?

PinkDefense is an affiliate of Walter J. Pink & Associates, PC and Pink Law Enterprises, the Pink family of firms founded by civil rights attorney Walter Jerome Pink more than 50 years ago.

How much does a criminal defense lawyer cost?

For most criminal cases we quote a flat fee after a free consultation, so you know the cost up front. The fee depends on the charge, the county, and whether the case is likely to go to trial.

What should I do right after an arrest?

Stay calm, do not discuss the facts with police, jail staff, or other inmates, and do not post about the case. Ask for a lawyer and call us at (832) 979-0882. In a DWI arrest, you generally have only 15 days to request an ALR hearing to protect your license.

Do you handle personal injury or business matters?

Those matters are handled by our affiliated practices. For personal injury, visit PinkInjury.com. For transactional and business matters, visit PinkBusiness.com.

Will I have to go to court?

In felony cases you must generally appear at each setting. In many misdemeanor cases your attorney can handle some settings, but you must still appear when the court requires it. We tell you in advance which dates you must attend.

Can my case be dismissed?

Sometimes. Dismissals can result from suppression of evidence, insufficient proof, a complaining witness who does not support the case, or completion of a pretrial intervention program. We evaluate those paths at the start.

What is deferred adjudication?

A form of community supervision where the judge does not find you guilty. If you complete the conditions, the case is dismissed, and you may be eligible for an order of nondisclosure. It still counts as a prior for some purposes, so it must be weighed carefully.

Can I get my arrest record cleared?

If your case was dismissed, no-billed or ended in acquittal, you may qualify for an expunction. Certain deferred adjudication and some convictions may qualify for nondisclosure. See Expunctions & Record Sealing.

Do you speak to my family if I am in jail?

With your permission, yes. We can coordinate with family about bond, court dates and documents we need.
